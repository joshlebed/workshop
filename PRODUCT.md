# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Friends and couples using Workshop.dev together. Solo and shared collections of
movies, TV, books, date ideas, trips, albums. Most use happens in idle pockets
of the day on a phone (in line, on the couch, in bed), with occasional desktop
use from the web build. Multiplayer is the norm: a list is rarely a single
person's tool.

## Product Purpose

A calm, opinionated home for the running lists couples and friends keep "for
later." Each list adapts to the kind of thing it holds (a movie list looks
like movies; an album shelf is fed from Spotify), but the surface stays the
same shape so the app is one app, not six. Success: a list is so frictionless
to add to and reorder that the existence of the app fades into the
relationship using it.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, warm, confident. Three words: considered, intimate, durable. Tone in
copy: short sentences, sentence case, no marketing voice, never breathless.
Reads like a thoughtful friend writing notes, not a SaaS product talking at
you.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards with hero metrics, big number cards, gradient KPIs.
- Productivity apps that perform "power user" with dense toolbars and tag
  systems (Notion-as-checklist, Linear-for-personal-life). Workshop is not a
  productivity tool.
- Glassmorphic, neon, dark-glow "AI app" aesthetics.
- Cluttered consumer apps that compete for attention with badges, gamification,
  and FOMO timers.

## Design Principles

1. **One surface.** Six list types share one screen shape, one row pattern,
   one header. Type-specific behavior hides inside, never out front.
2. **The content is the interface.** Chrome stays out of the way; the list
   itself does the visual work. No decorative containers around things that
   don't need them.
3. **Calm by default.** Restrained color, generous neutrals, color used to
   identify a list (not to decorate the app).
4. **Reduce, then reduce again.** If a piece of header text or a stat could
   be dropped without harming the task, drop it. Information density is
   earned.
5. **Multiplayer where it matters.** Member count, last-action attribution,
   share affordances are first-class; everything else stays quiet.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Pressables expose `accessibilityRole` and `accessibilityLabel`.
Color is never the only signal (sections also use labels and dividers).
Respect reduced motion in any new animation.
